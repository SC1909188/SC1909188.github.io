01 / The mission

Privacy is more than a settings menu.

Digital privacy concerns how information is collected, used, stored, and shared. Ethical decisions ask what should happen; laws establish requirements and limits that apply in particular circumstances.

⌖
LOCATION DATA

Your location tells a story.

Navigation and weather apps may use location information to provide useful services. Consider whether access is necessary, how long information is retained, and who can see it.

◈
INFORMED CHOICE

Permission should mean something.

People need understandable information to make meaningful choices. A long privacy notice may technically disclose a practice without making its consequences easy to understand.

⌘
ACCESS & SECURITY

Not everyone needs access.

Organizations should limit access to personal information to legitimate purposes and use appropriate safeguards to reduce unauthorized access.

⚖
ACCOUNTABILITY

Responsibility follows the data.

Companies and developers should consider foreseeable harms, explain important practices, and review whether their systems respect users.

03 / Knowledge base

Ethics and law: connected, but different.

A decision can be legally permitted but still raise ethical concerns. A responsible technology professional considers both questions instead of assuming that compliance settles every issue.

◎

Technology ethics

Ethics involves reasoning about fairness, honesty, harm, consent, accountability, and respect for people.

  • Is the collection necessary?
  • Would users understand the consequences?
  • Could a different design reduce harm?
  • Are the benefits and risks distributed fairly?
§

Laws and regulations

Laws establish enforceable requirements. Which rules apply depends on factors such as the information, people involved, organization, and circumstances.

  • What legal requirements apply?
  • Does a specific rule cover this activity?
  • Are required notices or permissions provided?
  • Are there restrictions on collection or disclosure?
Example: a navigation app

An app may need location access to provide directions. Ethically, developers should consider whether continuous background access is necessary and whether users can understand and control it. Legal requirements depend on the app's practices and applicable laws.

Relevant United States examples

Know what a law actually covers.

CONSUMER PROTECTION

FTC Act

Section 5 of the Federal Trade Commission Act prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce. Certain misleading statements about privacy or data practices may raise consumer-protection concerns.

Read Section 5 ↗
CHILDREN'S PRIVACY

COPPA

The Children's Online Privacy Protection Act and its implementing rule impose requirements on covered operators of websites and online services involving children under 13. It does not apply identically to every app or every user.

Explore the COPPA Rule ↗

04 / Quick reference

Words behind the decisions.

Use this glossary when discussing privacy, ethics, and software.

Personal data

Information linked or linkable to an individual.

Consent

Agreement to a specific practice, where applicable and meaningful.

Data minimization

Limiting collection to what is necessary for a defined purpose.

Transparency

Clearly explaining important data practices and consequences.

Retention

How long information is kept before deletion or disposal.

Third party

An outside person or organization receiving or handling information.

Encryption

A method of protecting information by transforming it into a form that requires the appropriate key to read.

Data breach

An incident involving unauthorized access, disclosure, or exposure of information.

Accountability

Taking responsibility for practices and their consequences.
